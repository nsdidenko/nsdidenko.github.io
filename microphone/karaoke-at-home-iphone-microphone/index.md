# Karaoke at home: how to use your iPhone as a microphone

You've got a speaker, a TV or a laptop with YouTube, and friends who want to sing. The only thing missing is a karaoke mic. Your iPhone can do the job, and setup takes about two minutes. Here's how to connect it, what to do about Bluetooth delay, and how to make your voice sound good over the music.

## What you need

- An iPhone.
- A speaker. Any Bluetooth or AirPlay speaker works, or one with an AUX input if you'd rather use a cable.
- Something to play the songs on. That can be the same iPhone, a TV or another phone.
- A mic app that sends your voice to the speaker live. We make one called [Bluetooth Microphone FX Pro](https://apps.apple.com/app/apple-store/id6745152403?pt=120670397&ct=seo-karaoke-en&mt=8), and the steps below use it. Most apps like it work the same way.

## Step by step: iPhone and a Bluetooth speaker

1. Turn on the speaker and put it in pairing mode.
2. On your iPhone, go to Settings > Bluetooth and tap the speaker's name.
3. Open the mic app. In Bluetooth Microphone FX Pro, tap the AirPlay button at the top, the one that looks like an antenna, and make sure your speaker is selected.
4. Start the song. Search YouTube for the song name plus "karaoke" and you'll find a version with the lyrics on screen.
5. Switch back to the mic app and tap the big mic button. Now sing.

If you can, play the music and your voice through the same speaker. With the song on the TV and your voice on a separate speaker, the two drift out of sync and it's hard to stay on the beat.

Hold the phone like a mic, bottom edge pointing at your mouth, about a hand's width away. That's where the iPhone's microphone is. If you hold it flat in front of your face like you're reading a text, your voice will sound thin.

## How to deal with Bluetooth delay

Every Bluetooth speaker has a delay between the sound going in and the sound coming out. It's usually a fraction of a second. When you're talking, you won't notice it. When you're singing, you might hear your own voice a split second late, and that can throw you off.

What helps:

- **Use a party or karaoke speaker.** Newer, pricier models usually have less delay than old cheap ones. It varies a lot between models, so if you have two speakers, try both.
- **Keep the phone near the speaker.** Walls and distance weaken the signal, and then the sound starts cutting out on top of the delay.
- **Disconnect other Bluetooth devices** paired with your iPhone, like earbuds or a watch that's playing audio.
- **Don't use AirPlay for singing.** AirPlay speakers load audio ahead of time so music doesn't skip, which makes the delay much longer than with Bluetooth. It's fine for a speech but annoying for a song.
- **Use a cable if your speaker has an AUX input.** A USB-C or Lightning to 3.5 mm adapter and an AUX cable give you the lowest delay. You can't walk around as much, but your voice stays on the beat.

If there's still a little delay, sing along with the music, not with your voice coming out of the speaker. Most people stop noticing it after the first song.

## Make your voice sound better

A phone mic through a speaker can sound flat or boomy. Three settings fix most of it:

- **Room preset.** Hall adds a soft echo that covers small mistakes, which is nice for ballads. Studio keeps your voice dry and clear, better for rap or fast songs.
- **Equalizer.** If the speaker sounds boomy, bring the bass down. If your voice gets harsh on the high notes, bring the treble down a bit.
- **Noise gate.** It cuts the mic between lines, so the speaker stays quiet while you wait for the next verse. Start on medium. On strong, it can swallow a soft first word.

Watch the level meter while you sing the loudest part of the song. If the bar maxes out, turn down the master volume or move the phone a little farther from your mouth.

## How to stop the feedback squeal

That high-pitched squeal is feedback. The mic picks up the speaker, sends it back to the speaker, and the loop keeps getting louder. Point the bottom of the phone away from the speaker and stand a couple of steps away from it. The noise gate helps too, because it mutes the mic when you're not singing.

## Quick party checklist

- Charge your iPhone. A live mic drains the battery faster than playing music.
- Turn on Do Not Disturb so a notification sound doesn't blast through the speaker in the middle of the chorus.
- Make a playlist of karaoke versions before people show up.
- Turn on Record in the app if you want to keep the best moments. Recordings save with whatever effect you used.
