# How to connect a microphone to a Bluetooth speaker

You've got a Bluetooth speaker and you want to talk or sing through it. Maybe it's a birthday toast, maybe karaoke, maybe a yard sale where nobody can hear you. The catch is that a Bluetooth speaker is built to play sound from your phone, not to take a mic. There are three ways around that, and which one works depends on your speaker.

## First, check if your speaker has a mic input

Look at the back or under the rubber flap on the side. You're looking for one of these:

- **A mic jack.** A big round hole, usually labeled MIC, often with its own volume knob. Party speakers like the JBL PartyBox line have one, and so do a lot of speakers sold for karaoke.
- **An AUX input.** A small 3.5 mm hole, the size of a headphone plug, labeled AUX or LINE IN.
- **Nothing.** Plenty of small portable speakers only take Bluetooth and a charging cable.

If you see a mic jack, you're in luck. If you only have AUX or nothing at all, skip ahead to option 3.

## Option 1: a wired mic

Plug a regular wired mic into the mic jack, turn the speaker on and bring up the mic volume slowly. That's it. A wired mic has no delay and never needs charging, so for a speaker with a mic jack it's the simplest choice.

Don't plug a regular mic into the AUX input, though. AUX expects a much stronger signal, like the output of a phone or a laptop, so a mic plugged in there will be very quiet or silent. To make it work you'd need a small mixer or mic preamp in between, which is more gear than most people want for one party.

## Option 2: a wireless mic with a receiver

A wireless mic kit comes with a small receiver that plugs into the speaker's mic jack. The mic talks to the receiver, the receiver feeds the speaker. Many kits for portable speakers include two mics, so two people can sing at once.

Before you buy one, check two things. The receiver's plug has to fit your speaker's jack, and it may need its own charging. Some brands also sell wireless mics that only work with their own party speakers, so read the listing to see which speakers it supports.

## Option 3: your iPhone as the mic

If your speaker has no mic jack, this is the way to go, and it costs nothing. Your iPhone already has a good microphone. A mic app takes your voice and sends it to the speaker over the same Bluetooth connection you use for music.

We make one, [Bluetooth Microphone FX Pro](https://apps.apple.com/app/apple-store/id6745152403?pt=120670397&ct=seo-connect-mic-en&mt=8). Here's how it works:

1. Pair the speaker in Settings > Bluetooth on your iPhone.
2. Open the app and tap the AirPlay button at the top, the one that looks like an antenna. Make sure your speaker is selected.
3. Tap the big mic button and talk. Your voice comes out of the speaker right away.

You can also play music from YouTube, Spotify or Apple Music on the same iPhone while the mic is on. Both go to the speaker together, so a single phone gives you karaoke.

The one trade-off is delay. Bluetooth always lags a little, so your voice comes out of the speaker a split second after you speak. It's no problem for talking. For singing, some people notice it. Our [karaoke guide](/microphone/karaoke-at-home-iphone-microphone/) explains how to keep the delay small. If your speaker has an AUX input, you can also run a cable from the phone to the speaker with a USB-C or Lightning to 3.5 mm adapter. That cuts the delay to almost nothing, and the phone's output is strong enough for AUX.

## Can you pair a Bluetooth mic straight to the speaker?

Usually not. Most Bluetooth speakers connect to one phone, tablet or laptop at a time and only receive sound. They don't have a way to take a separate Bluetooth mic on top of that. Those Bluetooth karaoke mics you see online usually have a speaker built in and pair with your phone, not with your speaker.

If a mic says it connects to a Bluetooth speaker, check the fine print. It almost always means it comes with a receiver that plugs into the speaker, which is really option 2.

## Which microphone is best for a Bluetooth speaker?

- Your speaker has a mic jack and you sing a lot: get a wired or wireless mic.
- Your speaker has no mic jack, or you need a mic tonight: use your iPhone.
- You care about zero delay and you have an AUX input: iPhone plus a cable.
